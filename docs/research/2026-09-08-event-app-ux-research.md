# Event-App UX/IA Research — Primary Source Survey

Research date: 2026-09-08. Scope: attendee-facing app, onsite check-in, organiser admin, visual design, and pricing/positioning for eleven event platforms, to inform redesign of an in-house attendee portal (QR-launched), crew check-in scanner, and organiser admin. Sources are vendor product pages, help centres, app-store listings, and (where linked) design-system write-ups. Third-party review sites were used only to locate primary URLs, never as the basis of a structural claim.

---

## Cvent Attendee Hub

1. **Attendee app** — Bottom tab bar confirmed directly from the App Store screenshots: **Home, Schedule, Exhibitors, More**. Home is a personalised dashboard ("My Event Profile") with a daily summary, featured-speaker carousel, and AI-generated recommendations/daily summaries (branded "CventIQ"). Schedule splits into two sub-views, **All Sessions** and **My Schedule**, with filter pills and search; sessions can be added to a personal agenda with conflict checking. "More" is an accordion of secondary content (Know Before You Go, Weather, Dress Code, WiFi, Floor Plans). Cvent Events is a native iOS/Android app (78.5 MB, iOS 1491335576). Login/SSO is supported and some events require login or are invite-only/hidden.
2. **Onsite check-in** — OnArrival supports staff scanning (phone/tablet/desktop), self-service kiosks, and mobile scanners; walk-ins can be registered and checked in on the spot with badges printed immediately; badges print wirelessly in real time across "a variety of printer and badge types"; the system is described as continuing to function "even with limited connectivity." Full-service "OnArrival 360" puts Cvent staff onsite to run the desk.
3. **Organiser admin** — Configuration is reached via Event navigation → Attendee Hub → Website and App → "Manage website and app," then a **left-hand navigation** with sections including Design (e.g., Live Display) and Features (e.g., Session Content, Featured Content). This is form/settings-panel driven rather than a freeform drag-and-drop canvas.
4. **Visual design** — App-store screenshots show a navy/dark-blue chrome with a green brand accent on the icon, card-based home feed, list-style schedule and exhibitor directory with a grid/list toggle.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Enterprise, quote-only; markets itself on Fortune 100 adoption (89%) and 24/7 support — squarely corporate/enterprise, not consumer.

## Bizzabo

1. **Attendee app** — Vendor copy emphasizes a "fully private-labeled" mobile app centred on personalised agendas, 1:1 matchmaking, polls/Q&A, gamification, and push notifications; the marketing pages did not disclose an explicit tab layout, badge/QR display, or login policy — a gap versus Cvent's app-store confirmation.
2. **Onsite check-in** — The **Onsite Command** app (native, listed separately on the App Store) lets staff run a "fully-branded" check-in experience, supports both self-serve and staff-served flows, QR-ticket scanning, session-access control, and wireless badge printing. Bizzabo also sells **Klik SmartBadges** — RFID/NFC "tap-to-connect" badges with real-time heatmap tracking — positioned for 500+ attendee flagship conferences; **Bizzabox** is an iPad-based rental kit (including printers) aimed at sub-1,000-attendee events. No public detail on duplicate-scan handling or offline mode.
3. **Organiser admin** — A no-code **event website builder** (templates or build-from-scratch) with custom CSS/JS/HTML escape hatches; organisers can import custom fonts so the event site matches brand.
4. **Visual design** — Bizzabo explicitly states its event sites should mirror "the product experience['s]... typography, layout, and tone" — i.e., one visual language spanning admin product and public-facing site.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Tiered from DIY rental kits up to full enterprise service; Gartner/Forrester-recognised; positioning is B2B conferences, field marketing, and enterprise programs, not consumer ticketing.

Sources: https://www.bizzabo.com/event-app · https://www.bizzabo.com/event-management-software/onsite-event-management-software · https://apps.apple.com/tt/app/bizzabo-onsite-command/id1616390164 · https://www.bizzabo.com/event-management-software/klik-smart-event-badges · https://www.bizzabo.com/blog/how-to-choose-best-website-builder-for-event-planners · https://www.bizzabo.com/blog/beautiful-event-websites-design

## Whova

1. **Attendee app** — Native app (App Store id 716979741) with **bottom tabs**: Agenda, Attendees, Community, and more; the separate web portal instead uses a **left sidebar** — the same product deliberately uses two different navigation shells per platform. Home page after login surfaces Agenda, Attendees directory, Community Board, meetups and session Q&A. The Community Board is an explicit, named feature for rideshare coordination, icebreakers, job posts, and lost-and-found. App-store screenshots (marketing-style, captioned) show a blue-accented, white-background, list-first UI ("Mobile Brochure: Access Agenda, Maps, GPS, and Announcement Alerts"). Registration is mandatory — attendees log in with the email used at registration, or via social login. No personal QR-badge screen was found described in the attendee guide.
2. **Onsite check-in** — Whova's **Onsite Kiosk** turns iPads into self-check-in/badge-printing stations: attendees tap the screen, enter email or scan the QR from the app/confirmation email (or search by name), see a confirmation summary, and — if printing is enabled — a badge prints automatically; a "restart or ask staff" fallback exists for bad data. Kiosk iPads must share a network with the printer. No published detail on duplicate handling, walk-in registration, or offline mode.
3. **Organiser admin** — Web dashboard: organisers upload an agenda spreadsheet once and it syncs across web, registration page, and app; registration forms support custom fields, conditional logic and branding elements, but the vendor's own copy does not claim drag-and-drop page building.
4. **Visual design** — Consistent blue brand across icon, marketing screenshots and kiosk UI; screenshots favour dense text lists over illustration.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Positions itself as "all-in-one," award-winning, and academic/association-conference-friendly as much as corporate; historically strong in the association/conference market rather than pure enterprise.

Sources: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/whova-event-conference-app/id716979741 · https://whova.com/pages/attendee-guide/ · https://whova.com/blog/kiosk-check-in-badge-printing/ · https://whova.com/event-management-software/event-agenda-center/ · https://whova.com/blog/upgrade-organizer-dashboard-agenda-importing/

## Eventbrite (Organizer + attendee app)

1. **Attendee app** — The consumer **Eventbrite** app shows tickets under a **Tickets** tab; QR codes render full-size on tap and can be shared or saved as an image for **offline** access at the door. Navigation is a **bottom bar on iOS**, a **top-left menu on Android** — an explicit platform divergence stated in Eventbrite's own help centre. No personal agenda/session builder — Eventbrite's attendee app is fundamentally a ticket wallet plus discovery feed, not a conference companion.
2. **Onsite check-in** — The separate **Organizer** app is used by staff with admin/owner or check-in-role permissions; scanning shows a confirmation message, and re-scanning an already-used or invalid QR throws an explicit error. Three scan modes exist: standard check-in, check-out, and "validate only" (shows validity without altering status). **Auto-check-in** covers walk-ins/door sales by checking tickets in automatically at the moment they're sold on-site. No native badge-printing is described (Eventbrite integrates with third-party badge/check-in tools for that).
3. **Organiser admin** — Grouped into three functional areas: **Event Creation** (ticketing/registration/payments), **Event Promotion** (ads/email/social), **Event Hosting** (check-in/contactless payment). Attendee data lives under Manage Orders / Attendee Summary reports, with configurable columns and CSV/XLSX export.
4. **Visual design** — Eventbrite runs a named, documented design system, **Marmalade**: 400+ tokens, two brand themes (legacy rounded-rectangle EDS vs. newer pill-button monochrome brand), and **light/dark/high-contrast** colour modes for each theme; 15 themeable React components built on a JSON→Style-Dictionary token pipeline, with a Figma library mirroring the codebase.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Free to create; low transparent per-ticket fees; flexible payout schedules. Positioning spans both self-serve consumer/community events and paid corporate ticketing — the broadest range of any product surveyed.

Sources: https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/783059/how-to-use-the-eventbrite-app/ · https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/741083/how-to-check-in-attendees-at-the-event-with-eventbrite-organizer/ · https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/855993/how-to-customize-and-export-an-orders-report/ · https://www.eventbrite.com/organizer/overview/ · https://www.tnflnt.co/work/eventbrite-marmalade

## RingCentral Events (formerly Hopin)

1. **Attendee app** — Help-centre text gives the full **bottom-menu** list: Notifications, Messages, Event activities (Chat/Polls/Q&A/People), Details, **Home**, **Schedule** (My Agenda + 1:1 meetings), and **Explore** (Stage, Sessions, Expo, Speakers, Replay, Floor Plans) — the richest documented tab set of any product here, reflecting its hybrid/virtual-event roots. A personal **QR code** lives under Profile and applies to hybrid/onsite ticket holders; scanning it at a kiosk both checks the attendee in and prints their badge.
2. **Onsite check-in** — The **Organizer app** offers a password-protected, customisable **Kiosk** self-service mode alongside staff-run scanning of QR codes sent in confirmation emails; on-demand badge printing is supported at the kiosk.
3. **Organiser admin** — The **Event Dashboard** has a documented navigation with **Design, Registration, Content** sections; the landing page can use a "Classic" layout (auto-populated from Schedule/Speakers/Sponsors/Expo data, limited colour branding) or an "Advanced" **drag-and-drop page builder** with four base themes, custom fonts, and full branding control.
4. **Visual design** — Not independently documented beyond the builder's theme/font system described above.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Positioned as an "AI-powered virtual & hybrid event platform" — its onsite features (kiosk, badges) are visibly grafted onto a platform whose primary DNA is virtual/hybrid, unlike Cvent or Bizzabo which are onsite-first.

## Swapcard

1. **Attendee app** — Marketed as a single "modern, fully branded web and mobile experience," with an AI assistant ("Sherlock") surfacing session/people/exhibitor recommendations. Attendees get a **digital badge with a QR code delivered by email or mobile wallet** for contactless check-in. Organisers can white-label the app (custom domain, own app-store listing, multi-event hosting under one app) and can toggle a per-user **Dark/Light appearance** setting that does not affect event branding.
2. **Onsite check-in** — Supports self-service kiosks, staffed stations (including dedicated VIP lanes), and attendee self-scan via QR/wallet pass; badge printing covers on-site, bulk pre-printing, at-home pre-print, and reprint-for-lost-badge flows, with per-attendee-type badge layouts and sponsor-logo placement.
3. **Organiser admin** — Feature set spans Registration/Ticketing/Payments, Content & Session Management, Exhibitor Marketplace, Onsite Self Check-in & Badge Printing, and Analytics as named modules (implying a modular settings structure rather than one canvas builder).
4. **Visual design** — Explicit light/dark appearance toggle at the platform level, described above, is Swapcard's most concrete documented design-system fact.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Published entry price of **$610/year per user** for the Event Engagement Platform (annual billing only, no monthly option); pitched as "revenue-first," targeting trade shows, B2B associations, and hosted-buyer/exhibitor-heavy corporate events.

## Brella

1. **Attendee app** — Brella's own attendee help centre documents the **Home screen** in detail: Stories (Instagram-style event/sponsor content), Welcoming Media, Event Info & Venue Map, **Spotlight** (time-boxed surfacing of upcoming meetings/bookmarked sessions/announcements, shown from 10 minutes before/after a session and 10 minutes after arrival), **Top Networking & Sponsor Picks** (an AI-matched shortlist an attendee can shuffle), and Featured Speakers. This is the most networking/matchmaking-centric home screen of any product surveyed — agenda is secondary to "who to meet."
2. **Onsite check-in** — Brella's own documentation states it has **no native onsite check-in or badge-printing module**: "you would need to make sure the check-in platform of your choice and the Brella platform are integrated." QR codes for badges are distributed via CSV export to a third-party badge/check-in vendor. This is a structural point of difference worth flagging explicitly.
3. **Organiser admin** — Not independently documented in primary sources found; Brella's own help centre is networking/matchmaking-feature-focused rather than admin-IA-focused.
4. **Visual design** — Not documented in primary sources found.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Positions itself purely as "the event networking and matchmaking app," implying it is frequently deployed as a bolt-on layer next to another registration/check-in system rather than as an all-in-one platform — confirmed by client listings (e.g., Informa Connect conference pages) showing Brella co-branded alongside a separate registration system.

Sources: https://help-attendees.brella.io/en/articles/180829-home-screen · https://help-attendees.brella.io/en/articles/180818-how-to-navigate-the-event-schedule-agenda · https://help-organizers.brella.io/en/articles/182820-how-brella-qr-codes-can-be-used-for-lead-scan-and-check-in · https://www.brella.io/event-app

## Webex Events (formerly Socio)

1. **Attendee app** — Home screen is called the **Lobby** — "a highly customizable landing page ... as they join the Event App for the first time," carrying countdowns, welcome messages, upcoming sessions/livestreams and sponsor/speaker highlights. Rather than a fixed tab bar, the navigation is a **configurable list of feature modules** an organiser turns on: Lobby, Agenda, Sponsors/Exhibitors, Speakers, Documents, Maps, Live Stream, Polling/Q&A, Game, Wall, Attendees, Announcements, Video Rooms, custom lists, web links, and **My QR Code** as its own nav item (rather than living under a profile tab). Agenda offers session browsing plus a personal **My Agenda** sub-view. Both Web App and Mobile App variants exist, and registration is required.
2. **Onsite check-in** — The **Onsite** app supports staffed, on-demand/distributed, or hybrid check-in models, plus a "Fast Track" expedited flow and self-serve kiosk mode; badges are built in an in-house **badge designer** supporting access-tier icons for staff/press/speakers, and real-time check-in/print analytics (reprint counts, printer usage) roll into one dashboard. Hardware is sourced via a preferred rental partner (Choose 2 Rent) or bought/owned by the customer; default badges print in black-and-white, colour requires an add-on.
3. **Organiser admin** — Not deeply documented in primary sources found beyond the modular content-list model above.
4. **Visual design** — Not documented in primary sources found.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Enterprise/quote-based (Cisco-owned since the Socio acquisition); support marketed as "24/7/365," positioning it for large corporate and association programs.

## Luma (lu.ma)

1. **Attendee app** — Luma is web-first: event pages are generated from minimal input (cover image, title, description, date, location) and automatically add RSVP buttons, calendar links (Google/Apple/Outlook sync), and sharing — no page-builder needed. A native app exists ("Luma: Delightful Events," App Store id 1546150895, 4.9★/18K ratings, Apple Editors' Choice) for discovery, hosting, ticketing, chat, and QR check-in, and it **supports dark mode** plus VoiceOver/voice-control/larger-text accessibility options. Attendees generally must sign in to RSVP/hold a ticket.
2. **Onsite check-in** — Hosts/co-hosts scan via the web scanner, iOS, or Android app (the phone's native camera app cannot be used directly). Two scan modes: **Standard** (opens a guest-details sheet, requires a confirming tap — and if a guest holds multiple tickets, lets the scanner choose which one) and **Express** (auto-checks-in on scan, for high-volume doors). Delegated **check-in staff** accounts can search/check in manually by name/email without full event-management access. A digital wallet pass "works even if the guest doesn't have the Luma app or an internet connection." Dedicated hardware scanners are supported for high-volume events. Check-in staff seats require the **Luma Plus** paid tier.
3. **Organiser admin** — No drag-and-drop site builder is exposed — the product's design bet is a single opinionated template that always looks finished, with brand control limited on the free tier and expanded (custom domain, removing Luma branding, branded URLs) on Plus.
4. **Visual design** — Vendor copy leans on evocative naming ("Delightful," "Vivid," "Stellar" events) over a documented design system; consistently clean typography and generous cover-image imagery are the visible signature.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Free tier is unlimited events/guests; **Luma Plus is $59/month**, adding paid ticketing, custom domain, advanced analytics, email tools, and 5,000 invite/newsletter sends per week. Positioning sits closer to consumer/community/creator events (meetups, launches, run clubs) than enterprise conferences, though it is increasingly used for smaller corporate/tech events specifically because of its low setup friction.

## Dreamory Blue Lobby

1. **Attendee app** — "Event portal" is explicitly a **personalised, post-check-in space**: seating lookup (highlights the attendee's assigned table), exhibitor-booth location lookup, schedule viewing, document access (certificates/handbooks/papers), contact capture by scanning other attendees' QR codes, Slido-powered Q&A/polling, and customisable feedback forms. The vendor's own pages do not state whether this portal is a native app, PWA, or responsive web page, nor whether login is required beyond the check-in step.
2. **Onsite check-in** — QR check-in with on-the-spot **name-badge sticker printing** (waterproof/tear-resistant/sticker material options) that includes name, company and seating/table number; deployable as staffed kiosks or "iPad standees" for smaller footprints. No published detail on duplicate-scan handling, walk-in flow, or offline mode.
3. **Organiser admin** — Feature set organised around the event lifecycle: **Before** (registration/ticketing forms, exhibitor/OEM/booth portal, API integration), **During** (event portal, business matching, attendee engagement, check-in & badge printing), **After** (reporting, feedback/surveys, lead retrieval/export).
4. **Visual design** — Marketing site uses a dark navy-to-indigo gradient hero with a single **orange** accent colour for primary CTAs ("Request a demo," "Book a demo"), bold sans-serif headlines, and rounded/pill-shaped buttons — screenshot-confirmed on the live homepage.
5. **Pricing/positioning** — Two models: a **subscription** for organisers running events frequently, or **per-event setup** for occasional organisers where Dreamory deploys/configures the platform each time. Regional focus: Malaysia and Singapore, with presence extending to Japan and Thailand; 600+ events delivered; client base spans corporates, associations and government — a regional full-service (not pure self-serve SaaS) positioning distinct from the mostly self-serve US/EU vendors above.

## Synthesis

### (a) Common patterns across attendee apps
Three things converge everywhere: (1) a **personal agenda** distinct from the full session catalogue — browse, then star/add to "My Schedule" (Cvent, Whova, RingCentral, Webex Events, Swapcard); (2) a **QR identity artifact** (ticket, badge, wallet pass) that is the actual entry mechanism, usually tucked under Profile/Tickets rather than on the home screen; (3) an **AI personalisation layer** (Cvent's CventIQ, Swapcard's Sherlock, Brella's match-picks) that is now standard messaging even where the underlying UX — a recommendation carousel — is the same across vendors. Login is near-universal; only the consumer-rooted products (Luma, Eventbrite) show a lighter ticket-first flow, and even they require an account.

### (b) Common patterns across admin dashboards
Admin tools split along one axis: **settings-panel/left-nav configuration** (Cvent, Swapcard, Webex Events' module toggles) versus **freeform drag-and-drop page builder** (RingCentral's Advanced layout, Bizzabo's site builder). Almost every vendor organises admin around the same three lifecycle buckets even when unnamed: pre-event (registration/forms), live (content/engagement/check-in), post-event (reporting/export) — Dreamory names these explicitly ("Before/During/After"). Attendee-list management everywhere reduces to a filterable table with CSV/XLSX export (clearest in Eventbrite's configurable-column Attendee Summary report).

### (c) Common check-in patterns
Three roles recur: **staff-operated scanning app**, **self-service kiosk**, and **attendee self-scan** via personal QR/wallet pass; the highest-end products (Cvent, Bizzabo, Swapcard, Webex Events) offer all three plus badge printing. Where duplicate-scan handling is documented (Eventbrite, Luma) it's an explicit error or a "which ticket?" prompt, not silent failure. A recurring **express vs. review** pair of modes shows up under different names (Luma's Express/Standard, Eventbrite's default vs. "validate only"). Walk-in registration is first-class, not an edge case, in every onsite-first product. Offline resilience is the least consistently documented capability — only Cvent, Luma (wallet pass), and EventNook ("offline mode with auto-sync") make an explicit claim, despite how central it should be to a crew-scanner spec.

### (d) Where products differ, and why
The split tracks each vendor's origin. **Onsite-native vendors** (Cvent, Whova, Dreamory, EventNook) treat badge printing and kiosk hardware as core, with real hardware-integration docs. **Virtual/hybrid vendors that added onsite** (RingCentral, Webex Events) graft kiosk/badge features onto navigation still organised around streams and virtual booths — RingCentral's large "Explore" tab (Stage/Replay), Webex Events' toggleable module list instead of fixed tabs. **Matchmaking-first vendors** (Brella, and to a degree Swapcard) build the home screen around "who to meet"; Brella has deliberately not built check-in at all, positioning itself as a networking layer that plugs into someone else's registration system. **Consumer-rooted vendors** (Eventbrite, Luma) treat the app as a ticket wallet/discovery feed, with none of the session-level agenda tooling the conference tools share, reflecting a single-session (concert, meetup) rather than multi-track use case. **Regional full-service vendors** (Dreamory) bundle on-the-ground staffing and per-event deployment rather than pure self-serve SaaS, which shows up as thinner public docs — features are sold via demo, not self-discovered.

### (e) Design directions for a small team running 100–2,000-person corporate events

1. **Fixed 4-tab native app (Home / Schedule / Directory / More)** — Cvent, loosely Whova. Predictable and reusable across events; the "More" accordion absorbs long-tail content. Trade-off: an app-like build is more maintenance than a small team wants per event, and the fixed shape resists a very different event shape (dinner vs. multi-track summit).
2. **Configurable content-module list instead of fixed tabs** — Webex Events. Organisers toggle modules (Agenda, Sponsors, Map, My QR Code as its own item) rather than hard-coding nav. Trade-off: flexible across wildly different formats, but the nav looks different every time, costing attendees re-learning and the team a config UI.
3. **Single opinionated template, web-first, no builder** — Luma. One clean event page, calendar sync, QR ticket; free to $59/month. Fast and cheap for a two-person team, and web-first sidesteps app-store review — relevant for a badge-QR-launched portal. Trade-off: weak for multi-day, multi-track content (no personal-agenda builder) — best for the simpler end of the range.
4. **Networking-first home screen, decoupled from check-in** — Brella. Puts "who's here" ahead of the agenda and treats check-in as a separate, swappable system — matching a portal/scanner/admin split into loosely-coupled surfaces. Trade-off: attendees wanting "what's next and where" must dig past matchmaking content.
5. **Onsite-hardened, offline-first kiosk + badge stack** — EventNook/Dreamory. Prioritises offline/auto-sync and on-the-spot printing over app polish; the two SEA-headquartered vendors are the only ones to foreground offline resilience explicitly, tracking with venue connectivity being a real, named risk regionally. Trade-off: less attendee-app personalisation than the US enterprise players, but the most load-bearing direction for a venue-based, ~100–2,000-person event where a bad Wi-Fi day at check-in is the highest-visibility failure mode.

For a small team in this size range, (3) and (5) combine well: a lightweight, web-first portal in Luma's spirit (fast, no app-store dependency, opens from a badge QR link) paired with EventNook/Dreamory's offline-resilient scanner discipline, borrowing Cvent's fixed-tab clarity (Home/Schedule/Info) for IA rather than Webex Events' fully configurable module list — more flexibility than a two-person team needs to maintain.
